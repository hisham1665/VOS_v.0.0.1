# Evaluation and saved-result analysis

## 1. Evaluation question

The available repository evidence can evaluate whether Medical AOS behaves as a
bounded, provenance-aware, failure-tolerant analysis workflow. It cannot evaluate
clinical accuracy on its own.

The relevant saved artifact collections are:

```text
tests/test2full/testfiles/
tests/tetingmed1/
```

The second directory is spelled `tetingmed1` in the repository. Its index is
`tests/tetingmed1/index1.txt`; the first collection is indexed by
`tests/test2full/testingdetails.txt`.

The artifacts consist of Markdown plans and reports generated by earlier runs.
They are not raw test assertions. Most of the first collection contains PDF or
other document inputs and explicitly reports zero images. Some second-collection
cases contain one or two image files, but most of those images have unknown
modality metadata and were not sent to a supervised screener.

## 2. Evidence classes and method

This analysis uses three evidence classes:

| Class | Examined material | Valid claim |
|---|---|---|
| Source inspection | Current Python modules and configuration | What the implementation is designed to do |
| Offline regression | Tests with synthetic inputs, stubs, and injected endpoints | Whether a bounded software behavior works under controlled conditions |
| Saved run | Historical plan/report Markdown | What an actual stored execution displayed, including operational failures |

The saved cases were reviewed in this order:

1. inspect the index and actual filenames;
2. identify each plan/report study ID;
3. compare history, input counts, document/image metadata, and report topic;
4. classify direct-image, written-report, laboratory, and mixed evidence;
5. record node failures, fallback text, warnings, degradation markers, and
   model/backend details; and
6. exclude mispaired artifacts from disease-level interpretation.

Filenames alone are not a ground-truth manifest. Study IDs, plan history, source
references, and the report's own input inventory are stronger identity signals.

## 4. Positive operational observations

### 4.1 Source-linked reports are useful audit artifacts

The stored reports preserve more than a final diagnosis-like sentence. They
include disclaimer text, patient status, triage, red flags, next steps, source
page references, findings, extracted document reads, image metadata, screening
status, warnings, node diagnostics, runtime plan revisions, and models used.

For example, the kidney-failure report links eGFR, albuminuria, potassium,
bicarbonate, PTH, anemia, renal ultrasound, and nuclear-medicine observations to
specific pages. This makes it possible to inspect the source of the urgent
status even though no direct image was supplied.

### 4.2 Cross-source synthesis can be coherent

Several cases show meaningful combination of heterogeneous written evidence:

- **Kidney failure:** renal function, proteinuria, ultrasound, scintigraphy,
  anemia, electrolyte imbalance, and CKD-mineral findings form a coherent
  document-supported account. This is the strongest first-collection example,
  but it remains document interpretation rather than image validation.
- **Liver cirrhosis:** HCV evidence, cirrhotic morphology, portal hypertension,
  and synthetic dysfunction are combined into a high/urgent result.
- **Brain tumour:** imaging mass effect and pathology are brought together; the
  pathology source should remain distinguishable from a narrative or classifier
  suggestion.
- **Hernia:** the report retains a source-linked differential and absent
  complication signs rather than presenting only a positive label.
- **Cholecystitis and appendicitis:** written ultrasound/laboratory evidence or
  inflammatory laboratory evidence drives the report while confirmatory imaging
  is recommended where direct confirmation is missing.

These examples demonstrate evidence organization and limitation handling. They do
not demonstrate that the extracted findings or diagnoses are medically correct.

### 4.3 Disagreement can remain visible

The nose-fracture report is a particularly useful operational case. The written
radiology report describes an acute displaced nasal fracture, while the direct
image read says the nasal bones are intact and describes septal/ala fractures.
The saved report records:

- mixed evidence for the nasal-bone finding;
- separate direct-image and written-report sources;
- an explicit model-disagreement warning;
- a likely hypothesis rather than unqualified confirmation; and
- dedicated facial CT/ENT follow-up to resolve the discrepancy.

This is the expected shape of a provenance-aware discordance report. It is not a
measurement of which reader was right.

### 4.4 Conservative handling of negative evidence is observable

The later stroke artifact reports that its evidence audit rejected a hemorrhagic-
stroke rule-out based on negative or limited evidence. The result is useful as a
demonstration that a negative test or incomplete source does not automatically
become an authoritative exclusion.

The earlier `docs/TEST_RUN_ANALYSIS.md` describes a separate older stroke run in
which all 13 document nodes failed. Those two observations should not be merged:
the later saved artifact demonstrates the conservative rule-out gate, while the
older artifact demonstrates all-node extraction failure.

### 4.5 The reports expose incomplete coverage

The first collection consistently says `Images: 0 (unknown)`. This is valuable
because the reports do not silently present written imaging reports as direct
image reads. The second collection usually labels teaching images `Unknown` and
states that they were not supervised-screened. The saved output therefore
exposes the difference between:

```text
an image file exists
the modality is known
a supervised screener applies
a direct narrative read completed
```

That distinction is central to interpreting the artifacts.

## 7. Failure patterns

### 7.1 Backend availability and provider behavior

Historical reports contain `model_not_supported`, TLS failures, rate limits, and
unavailable text endpoints. These errors dominate several cases. They are useful
for testing fallback and diagnostics, but they should not be counted as model
false positives, false negatives, or diagnostic misses.

### 7.2 Structured-output fragility

Visual verification sometimes returned a plausible narrative such as a
preliminary CT/MR/ultrasound review instead of the required JSON schema. The
correct interpretation is “limited unstructured reviewer evidence; no structured
verification findings,” not “verification succeeded” and not necessarily “no
finding.”

### 7.3 Synthesis fallback can preserve volume while losing reasoning

The fallback reports carrying 25, 30, 36, 44, or 58 findings demonstrate that
observations survived enough of the pipeline to be rendered. They also show that
large copied finding lists are not a substitute for an indication, balanced
differential, opposing evidence, or targeted next steps. A fallback that is
complete in count can still be incomplete in clinical reasoning structure.

### 7.4 Metadata and fixture integrity

Unknown modality prevents appropriate supervised routing. Misnamed or mispaired
fixtures invalidate disease-level aggregation. These are data/evaluation defects,
not model-quality results, and must be reported separately.

### 7.5 Over-strong headline risk

The hepatitis artifact is the clearest example: a `NORMAL`/`ROUTINE` headline
coexists with unavailable synthesis, an invalid status value, unsupported
backends, and incomplete verification. This is why the current design treats
empty/failed interpretation as degraded or indeterminate and floors triage from
structured findings.

## 8. What the saved outputs establish

The collections support the following statements:

- The pipeline generated persisted plan/report artifacts for heterogeneous case
  folders.
- Reports preserve source references, warnings, model/backend details, node
  diagnostics, and coverage metadata in many cases.
- Document-only synthesis can combine laboratory and written imaging evidence.
- Direct-image and written-report disagreement can be represented explicitly.
- Provider failures, schema failures, incomplete screening, and synthesis
  fallback occur in realistic execution artifacts.
- Fixture identity and metadata quality are material threats to evaluation
  validity.

## 9. What the saved outputs do not establish

They do not establish:

- sensitivity, specificity, AUROC, calibration, or predictive value;
- superiority to radiologists or another model;
- that a labeled case was correctly diagnosed;
- that a high/critical status was clinically appropriate;
- that a normal document-only report implies a normal study;
- that an image file was diagnostically interpretable;
- that a model was independent of the written source it summarized; or
- that recommendations are safe to follow without clinical review.

## 10. Recommended controlled evaluation

Before reporting disease-level performance:

1. repair or relabel the rib-fracture and bronchitis pairs;
2. create a normalized manifest from actual filenames, study IDs, and source
   histories;
3. retain the original input files and exact prompts, model versions, routes,
   configuration, cache state, timings, retries, and raw outputs;
4. separate direct-image cases from written-report/document cases;
5. stratify by known modality, body region, series completeness, and backend;
6. have qualified reviewers adjudicate every source-bound finding, discordance,
   differential status, and proposed next step; and
7. report operational metrics separately: node completion, degraded-run rate,
   screening coverage, verification completion, latency, retries, endpoint
   errors, cache hit rate, and resource use.

The earlier `docs/TEST_RUN_ANALYSIS.md` provides complementary observations from
the older `tests/testingdeseases/` folder, including an observed synthetic
cached rerun improvement from 6.49 seconds to 0.14 seconds. Those timings are
not comparable to the saved collections without equal inputs and controlled
backend/cache conditions.
